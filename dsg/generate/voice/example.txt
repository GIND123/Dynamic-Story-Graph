Nell cut the heel off the loaf and pushed the rest across the table. "Eat that before it goes hard."

Tomas didn't touch it. He was watching the window, where the rain had started again, fine enough that it only showed against the dark of the hedge.

"You said you'd be gone by noon," she said.

"I said I'd try."

She wiped the knife on her apron and set it down with the blade turned away from him, the way their mother used to. He noticed. She saw him notice.

"If you're waiting for me to ask you to stay," she said, "you'll miss the light."

He laughed once, through his nose, and pulled the bread toward him after all. He ate standing up, the way he always had, crumbs going down his shirt. When he was done he brushed them into his hand and dropped them on the plate.

"I'll write," he said.

"You won't."

"No," he agreed. He picked up his coat from the back of the chair, shook the damp out of it, and put it on. At the door he stopped with his hand on the latch, as if there were something else.

Nell was already wrapping the rest of the loaf in a cloth. She held it out without looking at him, and after a moment he came back and took it.
